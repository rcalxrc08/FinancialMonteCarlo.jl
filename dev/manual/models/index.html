Models

Processes and engines

Models come in two flavours:

  • processes (subtypes of AbstractMonteCarloProcess) describe a financial asset: they hold an Underlying and, combined with a zero rate curve, simulate the asset under the risk neutral measure. They are what you pass to the metrics;
  • engines (subtypes of AbstractMonteCarloEngine) are plain stochastic processes, with no notion of rates or dividends. Processes are built on top of them: e.g. a KouProcess computes its risk neutral drift, simulates a BrownianMotion, adds the jumps and exponentiates.
BaseProcess
├── AbstractMonteCarloEngine           BrownianMotion, GeometricBrownianMotion, SubordinatedBrownianMotion
└── AbstractMonteCarloProcess
    ├── ScalarMonteCarloProcess
    │   └── LevyProcess
    │       ├── ItoProcess             BlackScholesProcess, HestonProcess, LogNormalMixture, ShiftedLogNormalMixture
    │       ├── FiniteActivityProcess  KouProcess, MertonProcess
    │       └── InfiniteActivityProcess VarianceGammaProcess, NormalInverseGaussianProcess
    └── VectorialMonteCarloProcess
        └── NDimensionalMonteCarloProcess GaussianCopulaNVariateProcess, GaussianCopulaNVariateLogProcess

Single name models

All single name processes take their parameters followed by the Underlying:

ModelConstructorParameters
Black-ScholesBlackScholesProcess(σ, underlying)volatility
HestonHestonProcess(σ, σ₀, λ, κ, ρ, θ, underlying)vol of vol, initial vol, market price of vol risk, mean reversion speed, correlation, long term variance
KouKouProcess(σ, λ, p, λ₊, λ₋, underlying)volatility, jump intensity, probability of an up jump, rates of the exponential up and down jump sizes
MertonMertonProcess(σ, λ, μ_jump, σ_jump, underlying)volatility, jump intensity, mean and standard deviation of the normal jump sizes
Variance GammaVarianceGammaProcess(σ, θ, κ, underlying)volatility, drift and variance rate of the gamma subordinator
Normal Inverse GaussianNormalInverseGaussianProcess(σ, θ, κ, underlying)volatility, drift and variance rate of the inverse gaussian subordinator
LogNormal mixtureLogNormalMixture(η, λ, underlying)vector of volatilities, vector of the first length(η) - 1 weights
Shifted LogNormal mixtureShiftedLogNormalMixture(η, λ, α, underlying)as above, plus a shift

Constructors check the parameters (positive volatilities and intensities, valid probabilities and correlations, existence of the risk neutral drift, ...) and throw an AssertionError when they are not admissible.

Engines

EngineConstructor
Brownian motion with driftBrownianMotion(σ, μ)
Geometric Brownian motionGeometricBrownianMotion(σ, μ, x0)
Subordinated Brownian motionSubordinatedBrownianMotion(σ, μ, θ, subordinator::Distribution)

The drift μ can also be a curve, see Market data.

Simulation

simulate returns the simulated paths, one per row:

S = simulate(model, rfCurve, mcConfig, T)        # processes: Nsim × (Nstep + 1) matrix
X = simulate(BrownianMotion(0.2, 0.0), mcConfig, T)  # engines need no zero rate curve

Multivariate processes return a vector of such matrices, one per component.

Multivariate models

GaussianCopulaNVariateProcess and GaussianCopulaNVariateLogProcess couple single name models through a Gaussian copula with correlation matrix ρ:

GaussianCopulaNVariateProcess(ρ, model_1, model_2, model_3)
GaussianCopulaNVariateProcess(model_1, model_2, ρ₁₂)   # bivariate shortcut

The log variant applies the copula to the log returns of the components. See Portfolios and multivariate for how to price contracts on them.

Parameters

FinancialMonteCarlo.get_parameters returns the parameters of a model (or of a payoff) as a vector, and FinancialMonteCarlo.set_parameters! builds a new model with the given parameters. Together they make calibration loops independent of the model.

See Models in the API reference.
